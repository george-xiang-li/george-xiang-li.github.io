sections:
  - heading: The Problem
    body: |
      George wanted to explore everyday waste handling as an engineering problem. Traditional bins can create odor and hygiene concerns, and users may forget to clear them regularly.
  - heading: Prototype Concept
    body: |
      The prototype used a square, two-layer structure with automated disinfection-related functions and a reminder mechanism. George combined computer-aided design, 3D printing, assembly, electronics, testing, and presentation in the development process.
  - heading: Build Process
    body: |
      The process moved from identifying a real-life problem to researching possible solutions, designing the structure on a computer, 3D printing parts, assembling the prototype, testing functions, and presenting the result.
  - heading: Failure & Debugging
    body: |
      Several problems appeared during construction. The lid dimensions did not initially fit, some parts required sanding or redesign, and electronic functions such as the UV and alarm components did not always work as expected. George adjusted dimensions, reprinted parts, disassembled sections, checked components, and replaced parts when needed.
  - heading: What George Learned
    body: |
      The project helped George become more familiar with sensor-based devices, mechanical fit, iteration, and staying calm when a prototype does not work on the first attempt.
  - heading: Possible Next Version
    body: |
      Future development could focus on smoother internal partitions, improved appearance and stability, more reliable sensing, and stronger testing documentation. Any future claims related to disinfection effectiveness should be supported by appropriate safety review and controlled testing.
